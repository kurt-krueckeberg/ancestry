* *Why use it:* Outlines the tenant-landlord structures, the role of
  *Anerbenrecht*, and how peasant land-tenure maps to regional naming

* *Why use it:* Since Schaumburg-Lippe shares its borders and agricultural
  customs directly with Westphalia, this extensive article explains the
legal mechanics of the *Anerbe*, the protection of the farm's assets, and
the compensation of siblings.

* **GenWiki (CompGen): [Meierrecht**](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://wiki.genealogy.net/Meierrecht)

* *Why use it:* Details the tenancy laws governing *Meier* holdings,
  explaining the legal contractual handovers (*Meierbriefe*) that went
hand-in-hand with hereditary succession.

### 2. Archival Searches

* **Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv Bückeburg (State Archive Bückeburg)**
* *Why use it:* Bückeburg holds the original records for the Principality

contracts), and **"Erbauseinandersetzungen"** (estate

## Anerbenrecht Overrview

* **Gender and Birth Order:** To keep the farm intact, the estate usually
descended via primogeniture (to the eldest son) or minorat (to the
youngest son, a regional variation common in parts of Westphalia). If there
were no sons, it went to the designated eldest daughter, who became the
*Erbtochter* (hereditary daughter).
* **Payouts to Co-heirs (*Abfindung*):** The non-inheriting siblings did
  not get land. Instead, the law required the sole heir to pay them a
modest, legally regulated cash settlement or dowry (*Abfindung*), or
provide them with shelter and a place to work on the farm if they remained
unmarried.
* **The Widow's Right (*Altenteil* or *Leibzucht*):** The surviving spouse
  did not inherit the farm's title. They were entitled to a retirement
portion of the estate—usually a small dwelling on the property, specific
food rations, and firewood—while the designated heir (or the daughter's
name-changing husband) took over full management.
* **Intersection with the Hofname:** Because *Anerbenrecht* legally locked
the tenancy to the physical ground, the family name of the tenant was
legally subservient to the historical name of the *Stätte*. Under this
framework, if an *Erbtochter* took over, her husband legally became the new
tenant under the land contract (*Meierbrief*), forcing his change to the
*Hofname*.

There is a significant warning flag in the FamilySearch Krückeberg tree.
The Johann Heinrich Krückeberg “aus Berenbusch” who married Anne Christine
Elisebet Seeger “aus Evesen” on 14 June 1761 is identified there as the
Johann Heinrich Krückeberg who was baptized on 19 January 1744. If this
identification is correct, Johann Heinrich would have been only 17 years
and nearly five months old at the time of his marriage.

His wife, Anne Christine Elisebet Seeger, was approximately 25 when they
married. Her age can be estimated from her burial record of 29 August 1773,
which identifies her as “Anna Christina Kruckebergen, des Johann Heinrich
Krückebergs aus Berenbusch Ehefrau” and states that she died at age 36 as a
result of childbirth complications, “in Kindbett.”

It is possible that a 17-year-old male married a woman about 25 years old,
but that would be unusual enough that the identification should not be
accepted without corroborating evidence.

In addition, we have not yet established whether a 17-year-old groom in
Schaumburg-Lippe in 1761 could legally or customarily marry a woman about
eight years older than himself, even if his parents were deceased.

The 19 January 1744 baptism record does not by itself prove that the child
baptized as Johann Heinrich Krückeberg is the same Johann Heinrich
Krückeberg who married Anne Christine Elisebet Seeger on 14 June 1761. The
baptism record proves only that a father surnamed Krückeberg had a son
Johann Heinrich baptized on 19 January 1744. The entry gives no father’s
given name, no mother’s name, no village, no house number, and no
occupation. It therefore cannot be securely attached to the later Johann
Heinrich Krückeberg of Berenbusch merely because the child and the groom
have the same name.

This is a warning flag less because this one relationship is especially
suspect than because it exposes a broader weakness in building a family
line from church registers alone. The registers show events; they do not
always prove identity-continuity across events. At present, we do not have
independent census lists, tax records, wills, probate records, guild
records, poor-relief records, or other contemporary sources that can be
correlated with the church register entries. We do have some information
from Schaumburg-Lippe Rentkammer case files involving Krückebergs in
Berenbusch and Evesen, but not for the specific time period needed to prove
this identification.

For that reason, the 1744 baptism should be treated as a candidate record,
not as proof. The proposed identity of the child baptized in 1744 and the
groom who married in 1761 remains possible, but unproven.
